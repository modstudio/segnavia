The Harbor

The first light over the quay showed the ropes still wet from the night and the boats riding close together.Harbor notes, 1843, page 12. A watchman walked the length of the pier twice before the bell rang.

The tide turned against the hull and the lanterns of the harbor receded into a thin gold line, and the crew counted the miles by the creak of the mast while the cook named every island that had ever fed them, and the navigator kept a second book of winds that never agreed with the first, and still the same sentence of water went on under the keel: a long, unbroken paragraph of sea that began on one page of the log and finished on the next, carrying the names of the ropes, the smell of tar, the weight of the spare sail, the sound of a cup rolling in the galley, the map that would not stay flat, and the promise that the far shore was only another harbor with a different bell. By noon the wind had shifted two points and the cook served bread that had taken on the taste of the hold. Nobody spoke of turning back. The log kept the same dry hand.

The second watch found a crate of lemons and a letter that named no sender. They put both under the chart table and went on counting miles.The letter is in the appendix; the lemons were eaten.

Evening brought a low coast that looked like the one they had left, until the bell from shore rang a different note. The captain wrote that the paragraph of sea had delivered them, and that the next page would begin with landing.

They made the lines fast and stepped onto boards that did not move. A boy sold water in a tin cup. The navigator closed the second book of winds and did not open it again that night.

In the morning they bought rope, ink, and a map that claimed the next harbor was three days east. The cook said four. They sailed on the third day anyway, with the same unbroken sentence of water waiting under the keel.

The crew sang once, badly, and then went back to work. The watchman of this new pier walked its length twice, as the first one had, and the log took down the fact without comment.
